Methods & open-science toolkit#

Last reviewed: October 2026. Many of these resources were originally curated by Victoria Guazzelli Williamson during the 2021 ABCD Workshop. Suggest additions via an issue.

General methods and open-science resources, organized by the decisions you’ll face in an ABCD project. For ABCD-specific guidance, see Best-practice guides; for ABCD tools and packages, see Working with the data and ABCD tools & resources.

1. Plan & preregister#

ABCD data are already collected, so preregistration is about committing to your analysis plan before you look at your outcomes, and being honest about what you’ve already seen. The DUC strongly encourages it.

  • Template: OSF Secondary Data Preregistration, with its tutorial paper: Van den Akker, O. R., et al. (2021). Preregistration of secondary data analysis: A template and tutorial. Meta-Psychology. doi:10.15626/MP.2020.2625

  • Transparency with existing data: Weston, S. J., Ritchie, S. J., Rohrer, J. M., & Przybylski, A. K. (2019). Recommendations for increasing the transparency of analysis of preexisting data sets. Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. doi:10.1177/2515245919848684

  • Registered Reports: peer review before results (COS overview and participating journals).

  • Blinded preregistrations for review: create a view-only link (search “view-only link” in OSF Help).

  • Example: an ABCD preregistration from Monash University: osf.io/cjnh4. Search OSF Registries for “ABCD” for more.

  • Before you submit: the ABCD reproducibility checklist.

3. Choosing covariates & framing causal questions#

Most ABCD questions are causal at heart, even when the analysis is observational. Covariates should be chosen by reasoning about causal structure, not by habit.

  • Wysocki, A. C., Lawson, K. M., & Rhemtulla, M. (2022). Statistical control requires causal justification. Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. doi:10.1177/25152459221095823

  • Draw your assumptions as a DAG (directed acyclic graph) with DAGitty (browser or R) or ggdag (R) to identify which variables to adjust for and which to leave alone (mediators, colliders).

  • For race/ethnicity and SES specifically, see the equity section of Best-practice guides.

4. Nesting: family, site, and repeated measures#

ABCD observations are not independent. Siblings and twins share families, families share sites (and scanners), and participants are measured repeatedly. Ignoring this understates uncertainty.

  • Typical structure: random intercepts for family nested in site, plus participant for longitudinal data, e.g. lme4::lmer(y ~ x + age + (1 | site/family/participant)). Many analysts simplify (e.g., one sibling per family, or site as a fixed effect) when models don’t converge. Whatever you choose, report and justify it.

  • Site vs. scanner: ABCD’s DAIRC has advised using site for non-imaging analyses and scanner (device) for imaging analyses, rather than both (May 2020 ABCD newsletter).

  • Worked examples: Thompson et al. (2019) and Smith et al. (2023) in Best-practice guides. Packages (lme4, glmmTMB, gamm4, FEMA) are on Working with the data.

5. Longitudinal models#

Start with the ABCD-specific guides (Hawes et al., 2025; McCormick et al., 2023; King et al., 2018) in Best-practice guides. For specific model families:

Cross-lagged panel models (CLPM) and the random-intercept CLPM (RI-CLPM)

  • Hamaker, E. L., Kuiper, R. M., & Grasman, R. P. P. P. (2015). A critique of the cross-lagged panel model. Psychological Methods. doi:10.1037/a0038889

  • Mulder, J. D., & Hamaker, E. L. (2020). Three extensions of the random intercept cross-lagged panel model. Structural Equation Modeling. doi:10.1080/10705511.2020.1784738

  • Orth, U., Clark, D. A., Donnellan, M. B., & Robins, R. W. (2021). Testing prospective effects in longitudinal research: Comparing seven competing cross-lagged models. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. doi:10.1037/pspp0000358. An overview of the different model types (suggested by Sara Weston).

  • Code (R lavaan / Mplus): RI-CLPM tutorial site (Mulder) · OSF materials · RI-CLPM lavaan demo and riclpmr package (Flournoy)

Latent change score models

  • Kievit, R. A., et al. (2018). Developmental cognitive neuroscience using latent change score models: A tutorial and applications. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2017.11.007

6. Measurement & validity#

ABCD has many brief or adapted measures. Check that a scale measures what you think it does before modeling it.

  • Borsboom, D., Mellenbergh, G. J., & van Heerden, J. (2004). The concept of validity. Psychological Review. doi:10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.1061

  • Flake, J. K., Pek, J., & Hehman, E. (2017). Construct validation in social and personality research: Current practice and recommendations. Social Psychological and Personality Science. doi:10.1177/1948550617693063

  • Rhemtulla, M., van Bork, R., & Borsboom, D. (2020). Worse than measurement error: Consequences of inappropriate latent variable measurement models. Psychological Methods. doi:10.1037/met0000220

7. Effect sizes & inference#

With ~11,000 participants nearly everything is “significant”. The question is whether an effect is meaningful.

  • Funder, D. C., & Ozer, D. J. (2019). Evaluating effect size in psychological research: Sense and nonsense. Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. doi:10.1177/2515245919847202

  • Tong, C. (2019). Statistical inference enables bad science; statistical thinking enables good science. The American Statistician. doi:10.1080/00031305.2018.1518264

  • Devezer, B., Navarro, D. J., Vandekerckhove, J., & Buzbas, E. O. (2021). The case for formal methodology in scientific reform. Royal Society Open Science. doi:10.1098/rsos.200805

  • NIDA “Meaningful Effects” workshop (2020) (summary PDF)

  • ABCD-specific benchmarks: Dick et al. (2021) and Owens et al. (2021) in Best-practice guides.

8. Prediction & machine learning#

The most common mistake with ABCD is leakage: siblings, or participants from the same site, ending up in both training and test sets, or the same participant’s later visits being used to “predict” their earlier ones.

  • Split by group, not by row. Keep families together, and consider leave-one-site-out validation to test generalization across sites: scikit-learn GroupKFold (Python) · rsample group_vfold_cv() (R)

  • Stratified and other splitting strategies: caret data-splitting guide

  • Do all preprocessing inside the cross-validation loop (scaling, imputation, feature selection, and harmonization such as ComBat).

  • Expect small effects: Marek et al. (2022) in Best-practice guides.

9. Imaging preprocessing#

  • Esteban, O., et al. (2019). fMRIPrep: A robust preprocessing pipeline for functional MRI. Nature Methods. doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0235-4. fMRIPrep is used in ABCC derivatives.

  • Running fMRIPrep on a cluster without internet access (Singularity/Apptainer FAQ)
