# Best-practice guides (peer-reviewed reading list)

*Last reviewed: October 2026. Have a paper to add? [Open an issue](https://github.com/now-i-know-my-abcd/docs/issues) or use our [submission form](https://forms.gle/Qck8ifmuJXmuNWhq5).*

An annotated reading list of practical, mostly peer-reviewed guides for working with ABCD. If you only read one, start with **Saragosa-Harris et al. (2022)**. Preprints are labeled as such.

## Start here: practical & responsible-use guides

| Paper | Why read it |
|---|---|
| Saragosa-Harris, N. M., et al. (2022). A practical guide for researchers and reviewers using the ABCD Study and other large longitudinal datasets. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2022.101115](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2022.101115) | Written by trainees from the 2021 ABCD workshop. A checklist of analytic decisions, reproducibility, and equity considerations, useful for both authors and reviewers. |
| Simmons, C., et al. (2021). Responsible use of open-access developmental data: The Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study. *Psychological Science*. [doi:10.1177/09567976211003564](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976211003564) | Ethical and interpretive pitfalls of open developmental data, from consortium members. |
| Dick, A. S., et al. (2021). Meaningful associations in the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development Study. *NeuroImage*. [doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118262](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118262) | Effect sizes, covariate choice, weighting, and nesting in ABCD: how to interpret "small" effects. |
| Owens, M. M., et al. (2021). Recalibrating expectations about effect size: A multi-method survey of effect sizes in the ABCD study. *PLOS ONE*. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0257535](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257535) | Benchmarks for typical (small) effect sizes across ABCD measures. |
| Lopez, D. A., et al. (2024). Transparency and reproducibility in the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101408](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101408) | Audit of 549 ABCD papers, with concrete recommendations for what to report. |

## Study design, sampling, and weights

| Paper | Why read it |
|---|---|
| Volkow, N. D., et al. (2018). The conception of the ABCD study: From substance use to a broad NIH collaboration. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2017.10.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2017.10.002) | Why the study exists and what it was designed to answer. |
| Garavan, H., et al. (2018). Recruiting the ABCD sample: Design considerations and procedures. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2018.04.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2018.04.004) | School-based sampling, sites, and twins: the basis for modeling nesting and weights. |
| Barch, D. M., et al. (2018). Demographic, physical and mental health assessments in the ABCD study. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2017.10.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2017.10.010) | Rationale for the core non-imaging measures. |
| Heeringa, S. G., & Berglund, P. A. (2020). A guide for population-based analysis of the ABCD Study baseline data. *bioRxiv* (preprint). [doi:10.1101/2020.02.10.942011](https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.02.10.942011) | How the ACS-raked propensity weights were built and when to use them. |
| Gard, A. M., et al. (2023). Why weight? Analytic approaches for large-scale population neuroscience data. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2023.101196](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2023.101196) | Empirical comparison of weighted vs. unweighted ABCD analyses. |
| Feldstein Ewing, S. W., et al. (2022). Measuring retention within the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2022.101081](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2022.101081) | Who misses visits and why it matters for attrition bias. |

## Longitudinal modeling and missing data

| Paper | Why read it |
|---|---|
| Hawes, S. W., et al. (2025). Longitudinal analysis of the ABCD® study. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2025.101518](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2025.101518) | ABCD-specific guide to longitudinal models. |
| McCormick, E. M., et al. (2023). The Hitchhiker's guide to longitudinal models: A primer on model selection for repeated-measures methods. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2023.101281](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2023.101281) | General primer on choosing a longitudinal model, with a code companion. |
| Li, et al. (2025). Missing data approaches for longitudinal neuroimaging research: Examples from the ABCD Study®. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2025.101563](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2025.101563) | Alternatives to listwise deletion, demonstrated on ABCD. |
| Thompson, W. K., et al. (2019). The structure of cognition in 9 and 10 year-old children and associations with problem behaviors: Findings from the ABCD study's baseline neurocognitive battery. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*. [doi:10.1016/j.dcn.2018.12.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2018.12.004) | Worked example of mixed models with site and family random effects. |
| Smith, D. M., et al. (2023). Heritability estimation of cognitive phenotypes in the ABCD Study® using mixed models. *Behavior Genetics*. [doi:10.1007/s10519-023-10141-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-023-10141-2) | Modeling family relatedness (twins, siblings). |

Also see the longitudinal-modeling and cross-lagged panel resources on the [Statistics](../resources/statistics.md) page.
